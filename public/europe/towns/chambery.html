Chambéry.

Éléphants de Chambéry · France Division 1 · France

The Place

The historical capital of the House of Savoy — a small medieval city sitting between three Alpine massifs and France's largest natural lake.

Chambéry is the only entry on this list that is a real city — but a very particular kind of one. Population sixty thousand, metro area a quarter million, and a complete medieval old town clustered around the Château des Ducs de Savoie, the 11th-century fortress that served as the seat of the House of Savoy from 1295 until the dynasty moved its capital to Turin. The Sainte-Chapelle inside the château houses the Holy Shroud's most famous trompe-l'œil ceiling.

The city's calling card is the Fontaine des Éléphants, an 1838 monument to General Benoît de Boigne with four life-sized cast-iron elephants — and the source of the hockey team's nickname. Chambéry sits on a small plain enclosed by the Bauges (north-east), the Chartreuse (west), and the Belledonne range (south-east), with Lac du Bourget — France's largest natural lake — fifteen minutes north. Geneva, Annecy, and Grenoble are each one hour away. As an import you get a real city, real groceries, real culture — with the Alps on every side.

The Team

Éléphants de Chambéry

Founded 1972 by Dominique Flatry as the hockey section of the Stade Olympique de Chambéry. The "Éléphants" nickname has been used since 1991, taken from the city's famous fountain. Plays at the small but loud Patinoire de Buisson Rond.
