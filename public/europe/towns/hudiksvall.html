Hudiksvall.

Hudiksvalls HC · HockeyEttan Norra · Sweden

The Place

A Bothnian Bay coast town the Swedes call Glada Hudik — Happy Hudik — for its lively social life, with a surviving district of 17th-century red wooden fisherman's cottages along the canal.

Hudiksvall earned its nickname in the 19th century, when word travelled south of an unusually friendly little place that took its hospitality seriously. The town is the largest in Hälsingland, the historic Swedish province known for its grand Hälsingegårdar — decorated farm-estates inland whose painted interiors are now collectively a UNESCO World Heritage Site (seven of them, scattered across the province).

The town itself sits on the Bothnian coast just south of the High Coast — the Höga Kusten — UNESCO-listed for its post-glacial isostatic rebound, where the land is still rising from the sea by about 8 mm per year. The defining sight in town is Möljen and Fiskarstan: a rare surviving cluster of red-painted wooden fisherman's houses along the canal, untouched by 20th-century redevelopment. Hudiksvalls HC plays at the Glysishallen; Stockholm is three hours south by train.

The Team

Hudiksvalls HC

Founded 1978. Plays at the Glysishallen, the town's primary indoor sports venue. Competes in HockeyEttan Norra, the northern division of Sweden's third tier — a stable, community-funded operation rather than a wealthy boutique club.
