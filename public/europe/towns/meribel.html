Méribel.

HCMP Bouquetins · France Division 1 · France

The Place

In the heart of Les Trois Vallées — the largest connected ski domain on earth, where every building must be wood, stone, and slate by law.

Méribel was founded in the 1930s by a Scottish major, Peter Lindsay, looking for a French alternative to the Austrian resorts closed to him by the Anschluss. Lindsay laid down a single architectural rule that has shaped the resort ever since: every building must be in harmony with traditional Savoyard style — wood walls, stone bases, slanted slate roofs. The result is one of the most coherent and visually calm large ski resorts in Europe.

It sits at 1,450 metres in the centre of Les Trois Vallées, sharing 600+ kilometres of pistes with Courchevel, Val Thorens, Les Menuires, La Tania, and (since 1996) the Maurienne valley of Orelle. The HCMP Bouquetins — the "Ibex" — were formed from a 1990 merger of clubs in Pralognan-la-Vanoise, Méribel, and Courchevel, and play in the Olympic rink built for the 1992 Albertville Games. The Vanoise National Park lies on the doorstep; summers are quiet, winters intense and largely Anglophone.

The Team

HCMP Bouquetins

The current Hockey Courchevel-Méribel-Pralognan dates to 1990, when the older Pralognan club (founded 1964) merged with Méribel and Courchevel as Hockey Club Val Vanoise, later rebranding as HCMP. Plays at the Méribel ice rink, originally built to host hockey at the 1992 Albertville Olympics.
