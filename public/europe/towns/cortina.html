The Place

The Queen of the Dolomites — UNESCO pink limestone, the Tofane towering over a town that has hosted two Winter Olympics, sixty-eight years apart.

Cortina sits in a wide alpine basin at 1,224 metres, ringed by the jagged limestone walls of the Tofane, Cristallo, and Sorapiss. It is the most photographed mountain town in Italy, and rightly so — at sunset the rock turns pink, an effect locals call enrosadira. The town itself is compact, walkable, and unapologetically polished: espresso bars, ski-house ateliers, and a 19th-century basilica with a bell tower that anchors the main pedestrian street, Corso Italia.

The 2026 Winter Olympics are co-hosted here with Milan, the second time Cortina has held the Games — it staged them solo in 1956. As an import, you arrive in a town built and rebuilt for the world to look at. The Stadio Olimpico del Ghiaccio, the open-air ice stadium where Cortina has played since the 1950s, is one of the most atmospheric rinks in European hockey.

The Team

SG Cortina Hafro

One of the oldest and most decorated clubs in Italian hockey, founded in 1924 and competing in the Alps Hockey League since the league's formation in 2016. Eighteen Italian championships, the most recent in 2025.
