The Place

A Hungarian-speaking Székely town in the Eastern Carpathians, where 81% of residents are ethnic Hungarians and hockey is the civic religion.

Miercurea Ciuc — Csíkszereda in Hungarian, the name still used by the locals — sits in the high Olt River basin in eastern Transylvania, ringed by the forested Hășmaș, Harghita, and Ciuc mountains. It is one of the coldest cities in Romania, with winter lows that regularly drop below −20°C. Eighty-one percent of the population is Székely Hungarian — a distinct community that has lived in this region for at least a thousand years and speaks Hungarian as a first language.

The town's centrepiece is Mikó Castle, a 17th-century rectangular fortress built by Ferenc Hídvégi Mikó with corner bastions and a moat — now a museum of Székely culture. HSC Csíkszereda, founded in 1929, is the most successful hockey club in Romanian history with seventeen national championships and four Erste Liga titles. The Vákár Lajos Ice Hall holds 4,000 — and on derby nights, all of them. Cost of living is the lowest on this list; English is patchy outside the rink.

The Team

HSC Csíkszereda

Founded 1929. Romania's most decorated hockey club: 17 Romanian Hockey League titles, 15 Romanian Cup wins, 4 Erste Liga championships (2011, 2020, 2021, 2022), and the Pannonian League title in 2004. Plays at the Vákár Lajos Ice Hall, the loudest rink in Romanian hockey.
