The Place

A medieval town on the Brenner Pass with an arcaded main street so well-preserved it has been featured on the cover of National Geographic guides to South Tyrol.

Sterzing — Italian Vipiteno — is the northernmost town in Italy and the gateway to the Brenner Pass, the lowest crossing of the main Alpine ridge. Founded as the Roman camp Vipitenum in 14 BC, it was elevated to a city in 1280. The town's symbol is the Zwölferturm, a 46-metre clock tower that physically divides the New Town from the Old Town along Sterzing's painterly main street, lined with tall arcaded houses in white, ochre, and pink.

The Fugger banking family — bankrollers of the Habsburg Empire — kept offices here in the late 15th century to handle silver from the nearby mines, and you can still walk past the Fuggerhaus on the central square. Innsbruck is forty minutes north through the Brenner; Bolzano forty minutes south. The Wipptal Broncos play in the Weihenstephan Arena, named after the Bavarian brewery, and were Italian Serie A runners-up as recently as 1998.

The Team

Wipptal Broncos Weihenstephan

Founded in 1948. Three-time Italian Serie A2 champions (2005, 2009, 2011), Serie A runners-up in 1998. Playing in the Alps Hockey League since the league's founding. The "Wipptal" name refers to the broader valley around Sterzing on both sides of the Brenner.
