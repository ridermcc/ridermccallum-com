The Place

At the foot of Europe's highest peak — the Rothschilds built the resort to be a French answer to St Moritz, and never quite stopped.

Megève and Saint-Gervais sit a few kilometres apart in the Pays du Mont-Blanc, looking directly across at the white slab of the Mont Blanc massif itself. Megève was built in the 1920s by the Rothschilds — Baroness Noémie de Rothschild commissioned the Domaine du Mont d'Arbois in 1921, the resort's anchor — and it was the first purpose-built ski resort in the Alps. The town keeps the strict Savoyard architectural code: wood-shingle chalets, slate roofs, no concrete towers.

Saint-Gervais is the more authentic neighbour, a working spa-and-mountaineering town that hosts the cog railway up to the Nid d'Aigle (the Eagle's Nest), the launch point for climbing Mont Blanc itself. The Yétis du Mont-Blanc were created in 1986 from the merger of the Megève and Saint-Gervais clubs and won back-to-back French championships in 1987 and 1988. Geneva airport is roughly 75 minutes — the best big-city access on this list — and the Anglo expat scene runs deep on both sides of the Pays du Mont-Blanc.

The Team

Yétis du Mont-Blanc

Formed in 1986 from the merger of HC Megève and HC Saint-Gervais. Two French Ligue Magnus titles in 1987 and 1988 (the predecessor Saint-Gervais club won six more between 1969 and 1986). Plays alternately at the Saint-Gervais and Megève rinks; long history of imports from Canada, Czech Republic, Slovakia, and Latvia.
